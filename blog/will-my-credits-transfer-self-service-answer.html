"Will My Credits Transfer?" Is the Question That Decides Your Enrollment

Prospective transfer students ask it before they apply anywhere. If your answer is "apply first and we'll let you know in six weeks," you've already lost some of them to a school that answered faster.

September 23, 2025 7 min read EnlitEDU Team · Reviewed by Chaitanya Saripalli
EnlitEDU TeamWritten with registrars, evaluators and enrollment leaders. Updated 2026-09-09.
Reviewed by Chaitanya SaripalliFounder & CEO of EnlitEDU and founder of Anatta IT Solutions, which supports colleges’ student information systems. LinkedIn

Here's a pattern anyone in transfer admissions will recognize. A prospective student calls, or emails, or shows up at an open house, and the first real question, before cost, before program details, is some version of: "If I come here, how much of what I've already done actually counts?"

It's a completely reasonable thing to want to know. Transfer students have usually already paid for those credits once. They're not eager to pay for them again, or to add a year to their timeline. So they shop. And the school that gives them a clear, quick answer has a real edge over the one that says "submit an application and our office will get back to you."

What most schools offer today

Two options, usually. Either an advisor does an unofficial estimate by hand, which is accurate but slow, and doesn't scale past a certain volume, or the student gets nothing concrete until after they've been admitted. By then some of them have committed somewhere else.

An estimate is not a commitment. The fear that stops schools from offering this is "we can't promise credit before an official review." True, so don't promise. Label it clearly as an indicative estimate subject to official evaluation. Students understand the difference, and a good estimate still tells them what they need to know to choose.

What a self-service answer looks like

The prospective student enters their prior coursework, or uploads an unofficial transcript, and gets back three things:

  • An indicative mapping: which courses look like they'll transfer, and roughly how they'd apply.
  • A total: approximately how many credits carry over toward a specific program.
  • The gap: what's left between where they are and the degree.

All of it before an application fee, before a login, before a six-week queue.

The objections, and honest answers

  • "Our catalog changes." A rules engine handles that better than a filing cabinet of PDFs. Version the rules; the estimate reflects the current catalog.
  • "Advisors will lose control of the process." Advisors lose the part of the job that's doing the same 200 routine estimates a term. They keep the judgment calls and the actual advising.
  • "It'll be wrong sometimes." Occasionally, at the edges. A hand estimate is also wrong sometimes. The question is whether a fast, mostly-right answer beats no answer, and for a shopping transfer student, it does.

Transfer students aren't asking you to guarantee anything. They're asking you to help them decide. Those are different things.

Why it moves enrollment

Faster yield, because you're answering the deciding question while the student is still deciding. Better-fit applicants, because someone who sees they'd lose a semester of credit self-selects out before they cost your office a full review. And fewer unpleasant surprises at orientation, when the official evaluation matches what the student was already told.

That's what Student Degree Pathway is for - putting an indicative evaluation and a clear path to the degree in the prospective student's hands early, running on the same articulation rules your official process uses so the two don't contradict each other.
