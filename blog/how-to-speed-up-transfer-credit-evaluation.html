Transfer Credit Taking Weeks? Here's Why, and How to Fix It

Most transfer credit backlogs are a process problem, not a staffing problem. Here's what slows evaluations down and what institutions are doing differently.

March 15, 2025 7 min read EnlitEDU Team · Reviewed by Chaitanya Saripalli
EnlitEDU TeamWritten with registrars, evaluators and enrollment leaders. Updated 2026-09-01.
Reviewed by Chaitanya SaripalliFounder & CEO of EnlitEDU and founder of Anatta IT Solutions, which supports colleges’ student information systems. LinkedIn

A student submits their transfer application in October. By late November, they have not received a credit evaluation. So they enroll somewhere else. You did not lose that student because of your program or your location. You lost them because your evaluation process took too long.

This happens at hundreds of institutions every enrollment cycle. And in most cases, it is not a staffing problem. It is a process problem - specifically, a data processing problem that your evaluators are solving manually, one transcript at a time.

What Actually Takes the Time

Ask any transfer credit evaluator what takes the longest and they will tell you the same things: reading the transcript and entering course data into the system, looking up historical equivalency decisions, and finding catalog descriptions to match courses that have no prior decision. These three tasks represent roughly 70-80% of the time spent on a typical evaluation.

None of them require human judgment. They require data processing. And data processing is exactly what AI does well.

The 80% rule: If 80% of evaluation time is data entry and course matching, automating those tasks reduces your total evaluation time by 80%, without touching any decision that requires human expertise.

What Institutions Are Doing Differently

The institutions reducing evaluation times from weeks to days are not hiring more evaluators. They are separating data processing from decision-making. AI handles the extraction, matching, and suggestion. Evaluators handle the review, exceptions, and approvals. The process changes from doing evaluations to reviewing them.

Roane State Community College reduced their evaluation time from several weeks to two business days. The evaluators did not disappear - they shifted from data entry to quality review. The work became better, not just faster.

How to Get Started

The first step is documenting your current evaluation process in detail, not what it is supposed to be, but what it actually is. How long does data extraction take? How are course equivalency decisions made? Where do evaluations sit waiting? That map shows you where automation will have the highest impact.

Frequently asked questions

Manual evaluation typically takes 3-8 weeks. With AI-powered evaluation tools, institutions reduce this to 2-5 business days for standard transcripts, with AI processing completing in under 60 seconds. Roane State Community College reduced evaluation time from several weeks to two days using LioraAI.

Transfer credit delays are caused by: manual transcript data entry (45-90 minutes per transcript), course equivalency research, peak-season volume, non-standard transcript formats, and lack of documented institutional policies requiring evaluators to decide from scratch each time.

The fastest path is automating the data extraction and course matching work, which represents 70-80% of evaluation time. AI platforms like LioraAI extract transcript data automatically, suggest course equivalencies, and integrate with your SIS to eliminate post-evaluation data entry.
